2018 Kilauea Volcanic Eruption Landsat 8 Images throughout time
09/28/2026

March 27, 2018

The imagery, captured before the eruption, shows the yet-undisturbed landscape.
You can distinguish the island's vegetation, some built-up areas, the original
volcano extent (in brown), the coast, and the ocean.


July 17, 2018

This image is during the eruption, however with all the smoke you can't see underneath it. A
raster dataset can contain more than one band. For example, satellite imagery commonly has
multiple bands representing different wavelengths of energy from along the electromagnetic
spectrum. The natural color layers you’ve used above combine bands 4,3,2 displayed as red,
blue, green (RGB). This band combination allows you to view the image as it appears to the
eye. In this section you will now display and combination of 7,5,4 which is the shortwave
infrared (SWIR) band combination. This band combination has the property of penetrating
smoke, which will allow you to better see what is happening on the ground under the thick
smoke.


August 2, 2018

In the August imagery, you can see that the eruption activity is slowing down.


February 26, 2019

Six months after the eruption, you can see the land and the ocean clearly again. The lava flows, now cold, can be seen in brown.
